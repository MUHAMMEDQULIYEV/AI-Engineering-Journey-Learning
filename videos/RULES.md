# 🎬 Rules: how to learn from a YouTube video

Watching *feels* like learning, but most of it is gone a week later. Pulling the idea out of your head is what makes it stay.
These rules apply the methods in [`../roadmap/learning-methods.md`](../roadmap/learning-methods.md) to videos.

---

## Before you press play
- **Pick a video that fits the current block.** Off-plan curiosity goes to an ideas list, not the screen.
- **Write 1 question** you want the video to answer (e.g. "why divide by √d in attention?").

## While watching
- **≤30 min per sitting** (≤40 for concept videos). **1–1.5× speed, never 2×.**
- **Pause at each new idea and predict:** what comes next, what will this print, what shape is it?
- **Coding videos:** don't type along line by line. Watch a chunk, pause, then write it yourself.
- **Don't copy the slides** into your notes.

## Right after (video closed)
Write **3 lines from memory**:
1. The key idea, in your words.
2. One thing that's still unclear.
3. One predict question + your answer.

Then add a row to the table in [`README.md`](README.md).

## Next day
- **Rebuild it without the video:** worked example → version with half the lines deleted → blank file.
- From now on, the video is **lookup only**: re-watch one part when you're stuck, then close it.

## Recall
Put the topic on the spaced-recall ladder like any other topic: **1 → 3 → 7 → 21 → 60 days**.

## Video vs other sources
- **Libraries / APIs:** the docs and source code beat a video. Spike first, then read.
- **Papers:** read the paper first (abstract, figures, one key equation). A video is only a backup.

## 🚩 Red flags
- Watching at 2× and calling it done.
- Watching 3 summary videos of the same idea.
- "I'll just watch something" on a bad day → use [`../roadmap/bad-days.md`](../roadmap/bad-days.md) instead.

---

**Done =** 3-line note + table row + one small piece of code (or a predict question answered). That's the commit.
